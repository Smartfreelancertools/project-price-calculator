How to Price a Freelance Project

Pricing a freelance project is about more than multiplying an hourly rate by estimated project hours. A reliable fixed-price quote should account for the time required to complete the work, revisions, meetings and communication, project expenses, payment or platform fees, and the profit you want to keep.

This freelance project pricing calculator brings those factors together so you can create a more realistic quote before sending it to a client.

How This Freelance Pricing Calculator Works

First, enter your hourly rate and the main project hours. Add realistic revision and meeting time, then include direct project expenses. Finally, enter any payment or freelance platform fee and your desired profit margin.

The calculator adds your labor and direct expenses, then works backward from the desired final profit margin and payment fee to calculate the client price needed to cover the costs.

Why Revisions and Meetings Matter

Freelancers often estimate only the time spent producing the main deliverable. But client calls, messages, feedback, revisions, testing, handoffs, and project administration can also consume paid working time. Including that time gives you a more realistic effective hourly rate.

  • Estimate the main production or development work.
  • Allow realistic time for revisions and feedback.
  • Include meetings, calls, messages, and communication.
  • Add software, stock assets, hosting, materials, or other direct expenses.
  • Account for payment processor or freelance marketplace fees.
  • Set a profit margin that supports your business goals.

Hourly Rate vs. Project-Based Pricing

Hourly pricing can be useful when the scope is uncertain or requirements may change. Project-based pricing can be easier for clients when the deliverables and scope are clear. Even when you sell a fixed-price project, estimating the hours internally helps you check whether the quote is financially worthwhile.

How Much Should a Freelancer Charge?

There is no single hourly rate that fits every freelancer. Your rate should reflect your skills, experience, specialty, market, business expenses, non-billable time, taxes and financial goals. A rate that looks high compared with a beginner rate may be appropriate for an experienced specialist, while a low rate may become unprofitable if much of your working time is spent on sales, administration, revisions, or unpaid communication.

How to Avoid Underpricing Freelance Work

Before accepting a fixed-price project, estimate the complete workload rather than only the main task. Consider discovery, planning, communication, revisions, testing, delivery and follow-up. Then compare the expected net earnings with the hours you will actually spend. This simple check can reveal when a project price is too low.

Freelance Pricing for Web Development, Design, Writing, SEO and Marketing

The same pricing principles can be used for many freelance services, including web development, graphic design, UI/UX design, writing, editing, video production, SEO, digital marketing, consulting, virtual assistance and other professional services. The inputs change from project to project, but time, direct costs, platform fees and profit still matter.
